# DARPA

> **The bottleneck.** A single technically credentialed individual could commit hundreds of millions of dollars to a programme no service branch had requested, and kill it, without passing a peer-review panel or owning a laboratory that would lobby to keep it alive.

The reference case for **breadth handled by time-slicing** rather than by co-location — and the reference case for a working kill mechanism.

## Facts

Established **7 February 1958** by Eisenhower (PL 85-325, DoD Directive 5105.15), deliberately *outside* the service labs. Initial appropriation $520M. **FY2026 enacted $4.322B; FY2027 request $5.039B.** ~220 government employees, ~100 programme managers, ~250 programmes, six technical offices, plus several hundred support contractors.

**Governance.** The PM writes the programme, tested against the **Heilmeier Catechism**, approved by an office director then the Director; after approval the PM holds substantial control. Applicants for PM jobs are literally asked to submit a programme proposal with their résumé.

**Term structure — the load-bearing detail.** DARPA's careers page states a **two-year term extendable to four**; independent studies report typical tenure of **3–5 years**. Hiring runs through the **IPA Mobility Program** (1970; home institution keeps them on payroll) and **Section 1101 authority** (1998, permanent 2017) — direct hire of technical staff on limited-term appointments up to six years, exempt from civil-service rules and pay caps.

**No in-house labs.** "The PMs do not conduct research themselves. DARPA has no laboratories of its own." All work is done by external performers.

## Scope: how wide, and what holds it there

Broad across six offices — but DARPA does **not** solve breadth by seating a biologist next to a physicist. It makes the **unit of organisation a programme, not a discipline**: the PM assembles whatever mix of universities and firms the problem needs, and disbands the assembly when the programme ends. Cross-disciplinarity is **contractual and temporary, not architectural.**

This is the cheapest known solution to the breadth problem, and the most transferable one in this vault.

## What forces disciplines to converge

A **shared paying customer**. DoD's roughly $190B/year in research and procurement means a successful prototype has a buyer who will scale it. Everything downstream of that — the willingness of firms to join a programme, the seriousness of the milestones — follows from the customer's existence.

## Kill mechanism

**The best in this vault.** Insulation at DARPA does not protect the *project* — projects are intensely evaluated and killed constantly. It protects the **decision right**: the PM is not evaluated by peer review, and the PM's tenure is bounded so their career does not depend on their programme surviving. A PM who leaves in four years has no incentive to defend a dying programme; PMs are expected to *propose to end* their own failing programmes, and this "is not considered failure but rather part of DARPA's learning process."

**The exit ramp is the discipline device.** Post-term, PMs return to home institutions, often at higher level; many become research-centre directors. Because DARPA service is a career accelerant, PMs can afford to kill things. Copy the term limits without the career premium and the mechanism does not run.

## Verdict, and the structural reason

**Worked: term-limited individual decision rights + no owned labs + a buyer.** All three are structural; none is a person.

**Why the clones underperform** — relevant because ARIA, ARPA-E and ARPA-H are the nearest live comparators:

1. **No operational customer.** Sharon Weinberger: "They have a customer with the deepest pockets in the world." Energy, health and civilian agencies have no equivalent procurement arm.
2. **Scale mismatch.** The UK's proposed ARPA was £800M over five years against DARPA's ~$3.5B *per year*.
3. **Political precarity.** ARPA-E has faced "constant threat of being eliminated," which destroys the long horizon the model needs.
4. **It is a bundle, not a menu.** Azoulay, Fuchs, Goldstein & Kearney: the ARPA elements are *complementary* practices — organisational flexibility, bottom-up programme design, discretionary selection, active management — and adopting a subset "would be less effective than implementing them all together."
5. **No alumni network, no career premium.** ARPA-E "lacks DARPA's history, broad political support, traditions, culture or alumni network"; its programme directors serve three-year renewable terms and its technical support is outsourced.
6. **Counter-evidence, in fairness:** the National Academies' 2017 assessment found "clear indications that ARPA-E is making progress toward its statutory mission and goals." So "clones fail" is overstated — the fair claim is that clones fail to reproduce DARPA's **transition to deployment**, not its research quality.

## Counterfactual

ARPANET and packet-switched architecture on that timeline; satellite geolocation via the TRANSIT/GPS lineage; stealth.

## Against me

- **The convergence device is a customer with a procurement budget.** The manifesto's problems — global health, climate, AI safety — have no single buyer who will scale a prototype. This is the same gap that killed the Gates Grand Challenges' translation record (`gates-grand-challenges.md`). If the manifesto proposes DARPA-like programmes without naming the buyer, it inherits the clone problem.
- **The model is a bundle.** Borrowing the appealing parts — programme managers, high-risk portfolio — without hire-and-fire authority, term limits, no owned labs and an alumni career premium reproduces the label and not the machine.
- **It is not an institution that does research.** DARPA owns no labs. If the manifesto's whole point is a permanent home for researchers, DARPA is evidence *against* that form, not for it.

## Implication

**Q2 and the design of the kill mechanism.** The transferable pattern is: make the **programme**, not the department, the unit; give one person real authority and a bounded term; make ending things career-neutral or career-positive. If the ten departments become ten independently killable programmes with a shared operating layer, breadth stops being a claim and becomes an implementation detail — see `00-argument/open-questions.md`, Q2, answer-shape 3.
