# Astera Institute

> **The bottleneck.** Fund AGI architectures *not* based on transformers, plus the invasive neuroscience needed to derive them — a bet no VC will make (no near-term product) and no university can staff (needs a thirty-person industrial AI lab plus primate and human BCI work).

**The most direct empirical evidence in this vault on breadth**, because it is a controlled before-and-after inside one institution.

## Facts

Founded ~**2021** by **Jed McCaleb** (Ripple, Stellar) with **Seemay Chou**. Nonprofit. **$1B committed to brain-inspired AGI** (Forbes, March 2026) out of a ~$3.9B net worth, plus **$600M** pledged specifically to neuroscience; both signed the Giving Pledge in 2025. McCaleb's thesis: *"Most effort and research… is going in one particular area — transformers. [AI] would benefit by looking closer at the human brain."*

**The residency** — 12–18 months, fully funded, **$125k–$250k salary plus $0–$1.5M project budget**, access to Voltage Park compute (24,000 NVIDIA H100s plus reserved B200/GB300). Publishing openly, explicitly to attract mission-driven talent.

**Programme:** the **Obelisk** AGI lab; **Doris Tsao** recruited to lead a new neuroscience programme; plan to implant BCIs in mice, monkeys and eventually humans to map neural patterns and translate them into AI architectures.

## The narrowing — 23 February 2026

Astera announced it was collapsing to **two areas**: *intelligence (biological and artificial)* and *AI-enabled life sciences*, and restructuring so the foundation is separated from semi-independent technical divisions, each with a leader holding **CEO-like authority**. Divisions: **Neuro & AGI** (Dileep George, ex-DeepMind, scaling to 30 researchers in 2026), **Radial** (life sciences), and Foundation (ops, residency, strategic investments).

In their own words, from the blog post:

> "Over the last five years at Astera, we've tested different approaches to funding and building ambitious technical work. We've explored a lot of directions to figure out where we think we can have the most impact."

And, more bluntly, from the announcement thread on Astera's own X account the same day:

> "Over five years Astera has tested different approaches to funding and building ambitious technical work. **The biggest takeaway: the more focused we are, the better the work gets.**"

That second sentence is the finding. It is a five-year, billion-dollar, self-reported experimental result on the exact question in `00-argument/open-questions.md` Q2, stated by the people who ran the experiment.

The residency was **refocused**, not closed, to "better prioritize people and ideas where we have long-term commitment and in-house expertise."

**Note the second stated reason for choosing those two areas:** *"We've also chosen them because we personally know them well."* That is a different rationale from Convergent's bottleneck logic — **narrowness following founder expertise rather than problem structure** — and it is worth distinguishing, because it is the rationale most available to a young author writing a manifesto, and the weakest one.

## Kill mechanism

None during the broad phase. The February 2026 restructure *was* the kill — applied to the institution's own portfolio, by its founders, after five years. An institution that has to reorganise itself to end things does not have a kill mechanism; it has a crisis.

## Verdict, and the structural reason

**Mid-transition.** No shipped capability results found. The most informative fact about Astera is not an output but a decision: **five years of deliberate breadth, ending in deliberate narrowing.**

## Against me

This is the cleanest available data point against the ten-department structure, and it is recent — five months old.

An institution with a billion dollars, no funding constraint, no bureaucratic constraint, excellent recruiting and total freedom of design **chose breadth, ran it for five years, and then chose narrowness**. It did not narrow because of external pressure. It narrowed because of what it learned.

A manifesto proposing breadth in August 2026 has to say why it knows something Astera did not learn in five years and $1B.

## Implication

**Q2.** Pair with `arena-bioworks.md` (broad, $500M, dead in under two years) and `santa-fe-institute.md` (broad, forty years, contested results). Three independent institutions, three funding models, three time horizons, same direction. That is close to as strong as observational evidence gets in this genre — and if the manifesto still wants breadth, this is the set it must answer.
