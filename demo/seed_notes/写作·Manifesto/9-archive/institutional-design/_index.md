**What Branch B says, in four lines:**

1. **The ask should be sized so one person can say yes.** Russell and Einstein asked for a meeting and got an institution in two years. Bush asked for an agency and got a veto and a five-year delay.
2. **Falsifiability buys a real argument and costs a caricature you never escape.** *Limits to Growth* is the whole case study. If you make a checkable claim, put the failure condition in one sentence — because the caricature will fit in one.
3. **What survives is a portable phrase and one concrete image.** *Heuristik der Furcht*; the memex. Not the metaphysics, not the technical particulars.
4. **Argue from a structural change in the nature of the act, not from a value.** Jonas's table — what traditional ethics presupposes, what technology violates — is the cheapest and most convincing form a philosophical diagnosis can take.

## The cross-border cases, on binding

| case | what actually binds | asset | exit cost | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **CERN** | indivisible immovable machine + 7-yr exit lock + income-indexed dues + military exclusion clause | accelerators, Geneva | **loss of a national discipline** | worked |
| **ICTP** | one patron with a domestic statute; UN flags for neutrality; binds *people* via 6-yr associateships | buildings, Trieste | zero for users; total for Italy | worked |
| **IIASA** | a détente bargain in a non-governmental wrapper | rented castle, portable models | **one year's dues** | mixed |
| **ICIC / IIIC** | advisory charter + one donor state | archives only | zero | failed |
| **UNESCO science** | constitutional subscription, annual majority vote | **none owned** | one budget cycle | mixed |

**The generalization that survives all five:** durability tracks whether the institution holds an asset that (i) no member can replicate alone, (ii) cannot be physically divided, and (iii) sits under governance the host state cannot unilaterally seize. **Legal ingenuity buys survival; sunk cost buys adhesion.** IIASA had the first and not the second, and its founding coalition evaporated within twenty years.

## The documents

| document | ask | what it produced |
|---|---|---|
| [[bush-1945-endless-frontier\|Bush, *Science, The Endless Frontier* (1945)]] | **a named agency with a governance design and a budget** | NSF — after a lost fight, a veto and a five-year delay. The frame propagated anyway |
| [[flexner-1939-useless-knowledge\|Flexner, "The Usefulness of Useless Knowledge" (1939)]] | a named institution, defended by refusing to defend it | the IAS founding myth; and permanent immunity from evaluation |
| [[collison-cowen-2019-progress\|Collison & Cowen, "We Need a New Science of Progress" (2019)]] | **a field** | a movement, three organisations, a policy shop — no institution |

**Three patterns.** (1) **None of them opens by asserting.** Bush answers four questions he was asked; Flexner poses a paradox; Collison and Cowen cite a prior manifesto that worked. (2) **The specificity of the ask predicts the specificity of the result** — named institution → institution; named field → movement. (3) **Bush lost on governance and money and it still worked**, because the problem framing propagated where the design did not. That is the realistic upside case.

## What the whole folder says, in four lines

1. **Insulated funding is necessary and nowhere near sufficient.** Every institution that worked paired it with a concrete shared object that forces disciplines to converge, and an explicit mechanism for ending things.
2. **Breadth without a convergence device and without a kill mechanism produces atmosphere.** SFI over forty years, IIASA over fifty, ICIC over twenty-four, Arena over two. Astera ran it for five years with a billion dollars and narrowed itself.
3. **A shared toolkit is not a convergence device.** A shared artifact or a shared paying customer is. This is the single sharpest analytical distinction in the folder and it was not visible until the cases were laid side by side.
4. **The live counterexample is [[arc-institute|Arc]]** — broad, unenforced, producing. Its breadth is intra-domain and its mechanism may be selection rather than structure. **Revisit in 2030.**

## Backlog — the next batch, ordered by what they would settle

**For Q3 (cross-border binding) — the biggest gap in this folder:**

- **Human Genome Project** (1990–2003). Multi-national, and the Bermuda Principles made the *data release rule* the binding object. Closest thing to an indivisible **informational** asset. **Read first.**
- **ITER** (2006–). Indivisible asset, seven parties, and a cost and schedule record that is the honest counterweight to CERN.
- **Square Kilometre Array Observatory** (2021 treaty). A modern CERN-shaped treaty organisation — what did the drafters learn from CERN and what did they change?
- **CGIAR** (1971). Distributed international agricultural research centres; the best existing example of a multi-site, multi-country research network aimed at development, and a real record of both results and reorganisation trauma.
- **EMBL** (1974). A European treaty lab that is *not* a single machine — the test of whether the CERN mechanism works without a CERN-scale artifact.

**For Q2 (breadth):**

- **MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology.** Janelia's other model: no outside grant applications, most scientists leaving after 5–10 years, eight Nobel Prizes 1950–1980. Probably the highest results-per-dollar case in existence.
- **Max Planck Society**, and **Wellcome Trust** — the two institutions the original manifesto sketch most closely resembles. Necessary, precisely because they are the "you just described X" objection.
- **ARPA-H** (2022) and **ARPA-E** — live tests of the DARPA-clone critique in health and energy.

**For Q4 (the mechanism) — the highest-value unwritten notes in the vault:**

- **The compute divide, as a body of evidence.** Oxford Internet Institute (32 countries host specialised AI data centres; 150+ have none); "Compute North vs Compute South" (Lehdonvirta, Wu, Hawkins, AIES); the ILO's *Mind the AI Divide*; the World Bank Atlas AI chapter. **These numbers are public and have not been metabolised into a moral argument by anyone writing for a general audience.**
- **Existing transfer instruments that actually worked**, as models for what a distribution mechanism looks like: the **Medicines Patent Pool**; **Gavi**; **PEPFAR**; **CGIAR's germplasm sharing**; the **Montreal Protocol's Multilateral Fund** (the strongest case in existence of a rich-country-funded transfer with an enforcement structure). **Start with the Multilateral Fund** — it is the closest thing to a working answer to Q4 in any domain.
- **Narayanan & Kapoor, "AI as Normal Technology"** — needs its own note, because O6 in `objections.md` cannot be answered from a summary.
- **Kulveit et al., "Gradual Disempowerment"** — the constructive counterpart is opening #2.
- **UN Independent International Scientific Panel on AI** and the **Global Dialogue on AI Governance** — the live multilateral machinery, first sessions in 2026.

**For the diagnosis:**

- **Fast Grants** (2020). $50M+, 260 grants, and the movement's strongest evidence that instrument design beats funding volume.
- **NIH study section reform experiments** and the IFP–NSF granting-incentive work — the empirical base for a partition diagnosis, if that is the shape chosen.
