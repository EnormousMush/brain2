# Janelia Research Campus

> **The bottleneck.** A tool-builder could spend six years building a microscope or a calcium sensor that no study section would fund and no biology department would tenure them for, sitting ten metres from the biologists who need it.

The case that **copied Bell Labs deliberately, understood which part was load-bearing, and left the rest.** The most directly useful design document in this vault.

## Facts

Established **6 September 2006**, opened October 2006, Ashburn, Virginia. Founding director **Gerald Rubin** → Ron Vale (2020) → Nelson Spruston (2024). Legally a campus of the **Howard Hughes Medical Institute**, a nonprofit *medical research organization* — an operating research entity, not a grantmaking foundation, which is why HHMI employs scientists directly. HHMI endowment **$24B (2022)**, total expenses $853M (2022); Janelia was ~15% of HHMI's operating budget as of 2019. (Cost figures for construction conflict: $500M total project cost in the planning document, $300M in secondary sources.)

**It said out loud what it copied.** Rubin: *"Every idea we have here, I can tell you who we stole it from."* The founding planning document contains an explicit comparative analysis:

| model | what Janelia took |
|---|---|
| **Bell Labs** | groups of 1–2 plus a leader; no tenure; weakest 10% encouraged to leave annually; all internal funding; young group leaders; hard problems over publication counts |
| **MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology** | groups of 2–6; limited tenure (<25% tenured in 1972); most scientists stayed 5–10 years then took university posts; **outside grant applications not permitted**; excellent core support; 8 Nobel Prizes 1950–1980 |

**Design features.** Group leaders with **2–6 lab members**, target 24 at steady state, **required to be active bench scientists themselves**, initial appointment 6 years. ~20 **Fellows** — fully independent, not attached to any group leader, 5-year appointments. **~80 core support staff** — vivarium, sequencing, **instrument design and fabrication**, mass spectrometry, information sciences — with support space designed **~50% larger than typical** to absorb unknown future instrumentation. **HHMI funds all of it and no outside grant applications are allowed.** Groups capped at six, justified by the claim that people do not have meaningful interactions with more than about twenty others. Glass architecture, movable benches, a pub, a subsidised cafeteria, a 100-room on-campus hotel. Rubin's stated *"nightmare failure scenario"*: creating "another Whitehead or Salk Institute."

**2019 self-assessment (Rubin & O'Shea, eLife).** ~41 labs, ~45 group leaders, >350 scientists. Early-career group leaders funded for 2 lab members plus shared resources, argued to be equivalent to a group of 5 in a typical academic setting. **20% of group leaders hired directly out of graduate school**, with no postdoc.

## Scope: how wide, and what holds it there

**Deliberately narrow** — neuroscience plus imaging and molecular tools, one or two research areas at a time. **Research-area turnover is scheduled** in roughly 15-year programmes: Molecular Tools & Imaging; Mechanistic Cognitive Neuroscience (2017); **4D Cellular Physiology** (announced June 2022, launched September 2022).

The reason for scheduling it is stated plainly, and is the sentence to remember: *"without an opposing force provided by management, there is a slow, steady drift toward a more conventional environment increasingly focused on maintaining successful programs."*

## What forces disciplines to converge

A **shared artifact** — the instrument, the sensor, the connectome, the organism. Janelia is not broad across domains; it is broad across *skill types within one domain*. Physicists, optical engineers, software developers, chemists and biologists are all pointed at the same instruments and the same flies. The founding assumption, confirmed in the 2019 review: *"co-localization of tool-builders with those who need those tools would greatly speed up"* innovation.

**Janelia understood what SFI did not:** cross-disciplinarity is produced by a shared object, not by shared abstractions.

## Kill mechanism

Explicit and, unusually, *funded*. Reviews at 5 and 10 years, with outcomes designed in three roughly equal buckets: reappointment; reappointment with a required transfer to a host institution; a transitional appointment while seeking outside positions. After ten years scientists can compete for **"transfer tickets"** — an HHMI Investigator position elsewhere. **Non-renewed group leaders receive roughly $1.5M in transitional funding**; senior leaders get 7-year renewable terms with 3 years of transitional support. Review criteria explicitly include "contributions to collegial environment."

**The exit ramp is the underrated part.** Bell Labs could fire the bottom 10% because it had 25,000 internal jobs to absorb them. Janelia has 350 scientists and no internal absorber, so it **buys** the soft landing. This is what makes "no tenure" recruitable — many recruits gave up academic tenure to join.

## Verdict, and the structural reason

**Worked: narrow scope + a shared artifact + a funded exit ramp.**

Evidence: **Eric Betzig's 2014 Nobel** in Chemistry as a Janelia group leader; the first full-brain *Drosophila* connectome at neuronal resolution; GCaMP calcium-sensor papers with >3,600 citations; >270 research or open-source hardware licences; >5,600 aliquots of fluorescent dyes distributed to >900 labs; GAL4 driver lines to 1,500 labs.

**Acknowledged failures, in their own words:** (1) small labs **could not scale** tool efforts, forcing the invention of "project teams" staffed with specialists and run "much like biotech start-ups"; (2) the drift toward conventionality, quoted above; (3) an unresolved "tension between what is best for an individual scientist's short-term market value and what is best for science" over authorship in collaborative work; (4) you cannot change a person's working style after hiring them.

## Counterfactual

Modern GCaMP-based neural activity imaging on the current timeline; the full-brain fly connectome; part of the super-resolution toolkit. Concretely: the class of instruments too expensive and unpublishable for an R01-funded academic lab and too specialised for a company.

## Against me

- **It is narrow, and its success is attributed by its own leadership to narrowness plus a shared object.** Cited as a model, it is a witness for the prosecution on Q2.
- **The insulation is an endowment plus a rule.** $24B behind it, and a ban on grant applications. The ban matters as much as the money — allowing grants would reimport study-section time horizons — but the money is not optional.
- **No independent external evaluation exists.** Everything on "did it work" is self-assessment plus prizes and citations. Well-evidenced on outputs; under-audited on the counterfactual.
- **HHMI has cut researcher grants before when the endowment took a hit.** Even the strongest funding insulation in this vault is not unconditional.

## Implication

**Q2, and the design of the kill mechanism.** Three things to steal outright: the **scheduled research-area turnover** with a named opposing force (this is what SFI lacked); the **funded exit ramp**, which is what buys the right to have no tenure; and the **oversized core support** of instrument-builders as first-class staff rather than service personnel.
