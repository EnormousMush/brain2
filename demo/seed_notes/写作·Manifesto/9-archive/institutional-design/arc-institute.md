# Arc Institute

> **The bottleneck.** Give biologists eight years of no-strings funding and a twenty-person lab so they never write a grant application — removing the selection pressure of the NIH cycle toward incremental, fundable work.

**The live counterexample to Q2.** Arc is broad, unenforced, curiosity-driven, and it is producing. Any argument that breadth requires enforcement has to explain Arc — or explain why five years is too early to tell.

## Facts

Founded **2021** by **Silvana Konermann** (Stanford; Executive Director), **Patrick Hsu** (Berkeley; Core Investigator) and **Patrick Collison** — the three who previously ran **Fast Grants** together. Independent nonprofit. **$650M** initial; **2024 budget $82.8M**. Founding donors include Vitalik Buterin, the Collison brothers, Ron Conway and Dustin Moskovitz. Board: Nat Friedman, Reid Hoffman, Susan Li. Scientific advisory: Carolyn Bertozzi, Aviv Regev.

**Tiered funding:** **Core Investigators** — up to 20 people, **fully funded for 8 years**; **Innovation Investigators** — $1M over 5 years; **Ignite Awards** — $100k for 1 year. Partnered with Stanford, Berkeley and UCSF; Core Investigators retain university appointments. ~300+ staff, 2 major initiatives, 9 core laboratories, 5 technology centres.

**Output.** **Bridge RNA** genetic engineering (Hsu, *Nature*, 2024). **Evo**, a biological sequence model (*Science*, 2024). **Evo 2** — preprint Feb 2025, published in *Nature* **4 March 2026**, "largest fully open biological AI model to date." One-year adoption metrics: 200+ citations, 88,000+ GitHub downloads, 380 forks, 8M+ API requests, 100,000+ HuggingFace model downloads, 48,000+ dataset downloads. Validated outputs: 16 of 285 AI-designed bacteriophages propagated; epigenomic designs at AUROC 0.92–0.95. **STATE**, a first virtual cell model trained on ~270M cells across 70 cell lines, plus a public Virtual Cell Challenge.

## Scope: how wide, and what holds it there

**Nothing holds it at investigator level.** Explicitly "no-strings-attached," curiosity-driven. The only concentrating device is the **Virtual Cell Initiative** — a top-down cross-cutting moonshot layered over bottom-up labs. Scope discipline comes from **hiring**, not mandate.

That is a real mechanism, and it is worth naming as such rather than dismissing: *choose twenty people so well that you do not need a mandate.* It is also the least transferable mechanism in the vault, because it depends on being able to identify and attract those twenty, and on a donor base that will fund the bet.

## Kill mechanism

None at investigator level. Eight-year terms are long. What Arc has instead is a **shared moonshot that makes convergence attractive rather than compulsory** — the closest thing in the vault to a *voluntary* convergence device.

## Verdict, and the structural reason

**Worked so far, five years in.** Evo 2's adoption numbers are the strongest evidence: this is not just publications, it is a tool other people picked up, which is precisely the deliverable the FRO retrospective says is hard and non-automatic.

The honest caveat: five years is short, and both Bell Labs and Janelia looked good at five years too. The failure mode that kills institutions like this — drift toward maintaining successful programmes, in Janelia's phrasing — takes fifteen.

## Against me

Arc cuts *for* the manifesto's breadth, which is why it needs the most careful handling.

- **The mechanism might be "hire twenty exceptional people," which does not generalise into an institutional design.** Under the house rules of this vault (no personality explanations), that is a warning: if Arc's success reduces to selection, it does not transfer, and citing it as structural evidence would be a mistake.
- **Its breadth is within one domain.** Nine labs in biology is not ten departments spanning medicine, energy, environment and AI. Arc is broad the way Janelia is broad — across skill types inside a field. It is weaker evidence for cross-domain breadth than it first appears.
- **Donor concentration is the standing risk.** Ashlee Vance, on this whole category: "if funders lose interest or money-spinning products fail to emerge, funding can quickly be pulled." Larry Ellison withdrew a $115M Harvard pledge in 2005. And see `arena-bioworks.md`, which had $500M and died in under two years.
- **The talent-drain objection.** A senior Oxford academic on this class of institute: **"They are raiding us."** An institution premised on serving humanity should have an answer to the charge that it is redistributing scarce researchers rather than adding capacity.

## Implication

**Q2.** Arc is the best available argument that a broad, unenforced, well-funded institute can produce — with two qualifications that blunt it: the breadth is intra-domain, and the mechanism may be selection rather than structure. **Worth returning to in 2030**; if Arc is still producing at ten years, the Q2 answer changes.
