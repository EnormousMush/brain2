# Hans Jonas, *The Imperative of Responsibility* (1979)

> **What this is for me.** The most useful philosophical model in the vault, because it does the one thing a philosophical manifesto must do: it identifies a **structural change in the nature of human action** and derives an ethics from that change rather than from a value assertion.

If the manifesto is philosophical, this is the argument shape to study.

## The move

Jonas does not claim people have become worse. He claims **the nature of the act has changed**:

> "with certain developments of our powers the *nature of human action* has changed"

And then he shows, point by point, that traditional ethics presupposes conditions that modern technology has voided:

| traditional ethics presupposes | modern technology violates |
|---|---|
| action is **local** — the neighbour is its object | effects are planetary |
| action is **short-range** — consequences visible within a lifetime | effects run over centuries, cumulative and delayed |
| nature is a **stable background**, not an object of duty | the biosphere is now an object of human power, hence of duty |
| the **existence** of humanity is a given, not something at stake | extinction is now an available human act |
| ethics is **reciprocal** — between contemporaneous moral equals | duty runs one way, to beings who cannot reciprocate or claim |

**This table is the whole technique.** It is a diagnosis, in the sense `00-argument/diagnosis.md` demands: it names a structural change, it is checkable against the world, and the mechanism follows from it without a new premise.

## The reformulated imperative — verbatim

Four formulations, from the 1973 essay and carried into the book:

> "Act so that the effects of your action are compatible with the permanence of genuine human life"
> "Act so that the effects of your action are not destructive of the future possibility of such life"
> "Do not compromise the conditions for an indefinite continuation of humanity on earth"
> "In your present choices, include the future wholeness of Man among the objects of your will"

German original of the first: *"Handle so, daß die Wirkungen deiner Handlung verträglich sind mit der Permanenz echten menschlichen Lebens auf Erden."*

**Contrast with Kant, per Jonas:** Kant's imperative tests *logical consistency* under hypothetical universalisation — a thought experiment available to one agent in an instant. Jonas's addresses **public policy** and demands **actual material compatibility** with the concrete future existence of humanity. It has a temporal horizon; Kant's does not.

Three risk domains named: nuclear weapons, genetic engineering, destruction of the biosphere. **Note that a 1979 book about the ethics of technological civilization gets two of three right for 2026 and could not have named the third.**

## Two concepts worth carrying

**Fernstenliebe** — "love of the distant," extending ethics beyond *Nächstenliebe*, love of the neighbour. And **the parent–child relation, not the contract, as the paradigm of responsibility**: asymmetric, non-reciprocal, unchosen, and triggered by the sheer vulnerability of its object. *(This is directly relevant to any argument about "together" that has to include people who cannot reciprocate — future generations, or populations with no compute and no seat.)*

**Heuristik der Furcht** — the "heuristics of fear." Its function is **epistemic first, not emotional**: we lack a positive image of what a good human future consists in, but we can form a determinate image of what its destruction looks like. **The threat is knowable where the good is not.** One consults the *malum* to learn the *bonum*. Then the decision rule: *Vorrang der schlechten vor der guten Prognose* — priority of the bad prognosis, *in dubio pro malo* — justified by asymmetric stakes: where the potential damage includes extinction, magnitude dominates probability.

Robert Spaemann, in the 1987 Peace Prize laudatio: the book is *"consciously and explicitly opposed to the Principle of Hope"* (Bloch's *Prinzip Hoffnung*) — **care, not hope, must henceforth guide human action on earth.**

## Influence

- Micha Werner calls it **"perhaps the most widely read moral-philosophical book of the postwar period in the German-speaking world."** The Jonasian formulae remain "omnipresent" in bioethics, including in Habermas.
- **Friedenspreis des Deutschen Buchhandels, 11 October 1987** — Germany's most visible non-fiction prize, awarded to a work of technology ethics.
- A 2020 Suhrkamp reissue carries an afterword by **Robert Habeck**, later German Vice-Chancellor.
- Concrete bioethical positions traceable to it: researcher self-restraint over external control; rejection of brain-death criteria for organ harvesting; genetic screening permissible for therapy but not enhancement.

**And the reason it lasted is worth stating precisely: the vocabulary survived the collapse of its own metaphysics.** Jonas grounded the imperative in a speculative philosophy of nature that almost nobody accepts. *Heuristik der Furcht* and *Vorrang der schlechten Prognose* are used every day by people who have never read the ontology. **A portable phrase outlives the argument that produced it.**

## What to steal

- **Argue from a structural change in the nature of the act, not from a value.** "Our powers have outgrown the ethics built for them" is checkable. "We should care about the future" is not.
- **Build the table.** Presuppositions on the left, violations on the right. It is the cheapest and most convincing form a philosophical diagnosis can take.
- **Coin two or three portable phrases and make them do work in the text.** They are what survives.
- **Take the asymmetry seriously.** The parent–child paradigm is the strongest available answer to "why do we owe anything to people who cannot reciprocate," which is the hard part of any "together" claim.

## Against me

- **It produced no institution and asked nobody to do anything specific.** Enormous conceptual influence, zero organisational output. If the manifesto wants an institution, Jonas is a model for the argument and not for the ask.
- **Its metaphysics is unusable and it succeeded anyway.** That is a hopeful finding and a dangerous one: it can license writing a foundation nobody will accept, on the bet that the vocabulary escapes. Most such bets lose.
- **The precautionary structure has a well-known cost.** *In dubio pro malo* applied to a general-purpose technology counsels against actions whose benefits are also enormous and also distributed. A manifesto about progressing *together* cannot simply adopt it — and must say why not.
- **Criticisms exist and should be read before leaning on him** (surveyed in Werner 2003).
