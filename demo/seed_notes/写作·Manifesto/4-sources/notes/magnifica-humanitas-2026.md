# *Magnifica Humanitas* (2026)

> **Direct competitor.** The most developed existing statement of "humanity advances together" in the AI context — published ten weeks before this vault was started, running to 42,000 words, with two thousand years of moral vocabulary behind it.

## What it says

Dated **15 May 2026**, released around 25 May. Core claims: **human dignity is not surpassable**; AI is **"never neutral."**

**The governing metaphor is the best thing in it: the Tower of Babel against Nehemiah's wall.** Babel is concentrated, homogenised, centrally directed building. Nehemiah's wall is distributed building, where each family repairs the section in front of its own house and is accountable to its neighbours for it. **That distinction — concentrated homogenised control versus distributed community-accountable construction — is a genuinely good frame, and it is currently locked inside a religious document.**

Concrete asks: inclusion of affected communities in design; protection from **digital colonisation**; algorithmic recourse; a warning about **"new forms of slavery"** in supply chains and content-moderation labour.

Humanity is framed through **communion, solidarity, subsidiarity and the common good** — i.e. as one family. This is, at present, **the most developed vocabulary anywhere for humanity-as-one in the AI context.**

**One detail worth more than it first appears:** the encyclical was **promulgated on 25 May 2026 at a presentation the Pope gave alongside an Anthropic co-founder.** A papal document on AI, presented with a frontier-lab founder in the room. Whatever else that is, it is evidence about who currently gets to speak on this subject and who confers legitimacy on whom.

## Reception

Praised in the secular press. Ignored in Silicon Valley. Split among Catholics.

**The critique that matters most here is Brookings': even this document does not specify affirmative mechanisms for distributing AI benefits to poorer nations. It prevents harm rather than distributing gain.**

That is the same gap as Amodei's, arrived at from the opposite direction — one from technological optimism, one from moral theology. **Two of the most serious documents in the space converge on the same hole.**

## Against me

- **On the stated centre of gravity, this document got there first, at scale, with better moral architecture and infinitely more standing.** Any manifesto about humanity advancing together in the AI age must be able to say what it adds.
- **What it adds is available and specific:** the mechanism the encyclical does not supply, and a secular vocabulary the encyclical cannot use. Those are two distinct contributions and either would justify the piece.
- **Its reception is the cautionary half.** The most morally serious document in the space was ignored by the people building the technology. Moral seriousness does not buy attention from the actors who matter. Plan for that rather than being surprised by it.

## Implication

**Q1, and the intent file.** Opening #3 in `_ai-era-landscape.md` — the secular translation — runs directly through this document. The Babel/Nehemiah frame is worth studying closely and worth *not* borrowing without attribution.
