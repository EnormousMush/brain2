# We Need a New Science of Progress — Collison & Cowen (2019)

> **What this is for me.** The closest living comparable, the movement whose venues I am targeting, and the clearest evidence about what a diffuse ask produces.

## Position the authors wrote from

A billionaire founder and a well-known economist, both with existing platforms. Neither held an institutional position that obliged them to write it. Compare Bush (asked by a president) and Flexner (running the institution he was defending) — this is the genre operating without institutional standing, which is the situation most relevant here.

## How it opens

With **genre self-awareness** — by citing a prior manifesto that successfully founded an institution:

> "In 1861, the American scientist and educator William Barton Rogers published a manifesto calling for a new kind of research institution. Recognizing the 'daily increasing proofs of the happy influence of scientific culture on the industry and the civilization of the nations,' and the growing importance of what he called 'Industrial Arts,' he proposed a new organization dedicated to practical knowledge. He named it the Massachusetts Institute of Technology."

It establishes the genre it is operating in before making any claim. Worth noting that all three exemplars in this vault open by *not* asserting: Bush answers four questions, Flexner poses a paradox, Collison and Cowen cite a predecessor.

## The argument, in five steps

1. **Historical precedent.** Nineteenth-century reformers deliberately engineered the institutional conditions for advancement. Progress has been consciously designed before.
2. **The gap.** *"Progress itself is understudied… there is no broad-based intellectual movement focused on understanding the dynamics of progress, or targeting the deeper goal of speeding it up."*
3. **Diagnosis — fragmentation, not absence.** *"Plenty of existing scholarship touches on these topics, but it takes place in a highly fragmented fashion"* across economics, innovation studies, history, management and the sociology of science. **No one owns the question.**
4. **The naming argument.** Fields become productive when named; they cite "climate science" and "Keynesian economics" as precedents where designating a field refocused effort. *"We suggest inaugurating the discipline of 'Progress Studies.'"*
5. **The normative differentiator.** It should be **prescriptive, not descriptive** — *"closer to medicine than biology: The goal is to treat, not merely to understand."* Success is measured by interventions institutions actually adopt.

**Note step 3.** That is a *partition* diagnosis — the problem is that the question falls between existing fields — and it is the closest published relative of the diagnosis shape sketched in `00-argument/diagnosis.md`. Read it carefully before writing that sentence, because the risk of restating it is high.

## The concrete ask

A **field**, not an agency. Named agenda items: talent identification and training; management practice effectiveness; organisational trust and delegation; science funding mechanisms and researcher autonomy; the demographics of who receives support. **No dollar figure, no proposed agency, no governance design.** Weaker than Bush's ask, stronger than Flexner's.

## What actually happened

| entity | founded | detail |
|---|---|---|
| **The Roots of Progress Institute** | blog ~2019, nonprofit 2021 | Jason Crawford. A 2–4 year fellowship, a Progress in Medicine summer programme, and the Progress Conference (2026 expecting ~400 attendees) |
| **Works in Progress** | 2020 | Bowman, Dattani, Southwood, Whitaker. **Acquired by Stripe Press in 2022** — a direct author-to-movement funding link |
| **Institute for Progress** | 2021 or 2022 (sources conflict) | Alec Stapp & Caleb Watney. 17 full-time staff, 9 non-resident senior fellows. Metascience, high-skilled immigration, biosecurity, emerging technology. Works with NSF on granting-incentive experiments |

**Correction to a common claim:** **Emergent Ventures launched in 2018 and therefore predates the essay.** It is not downstream of it. Its COVID spin-off **Fast Grants** (2020) is the one with hard numbers — **over $50M raised, 260 grants** — and is the movement's strongest single proof that a differently designed funding instrument can beat the incumbent on speed.

**The honest reading:** the measurable effect is *a funded intellectual ecosystem and a policy shop*, not a change in the rate of progress. Real but modest — and the same **category** of return the SDGs achieved. A diffuse ask produced a diffuse result.

## What a critic says

- **Historians of science** (The Conversation, 2019): the field already exists and has spent decades establishing that "progress" is **"a situated and often interested claim about human efforts, not a natural good or a divine gift. It needs critical assessment, not headlong zeal."** "Successful" and "brilliant" have no neutral meaning and have historically masked colonial expropriation and social exclusion. Evidence cited: Eric Ash on seventeenth-century English fen drainage; Anya Zilberstein on eighteenth-century colonisers welcoming anthropogenic climate change.
- **Distributional critique:** the movement measures aggregate growth and neglects inequality. Its internal answer is supply-side progressivism and housing reform.
- **The evidence base is contested.** The empirical claim the movement leans on most — that science is getting less disruptive — is under direct methodological attack. **Macher, Rutzer & Weder** show that correcting for methodological choices can make the measured decline in disruptive patents disappear or reverse; **Bornmann, Ettl & Leibel** (*EMBO Reports*, 2024) find limited convergence between disruption metrics and expert assessment. **If the stagnation premise is unstable, the urgency premise is too** — worth knowing before building an argument on it.
- **Cowen pre-empted the "you ignore prior work" objection** on Marginal Revolution the following day, noting antecedents are acknowledged across nine sections. Useful for showing the debate was joined.

## What to steal

- **Open by citing a manifesto that worked.** It positions the document in a genre with a track record and pre-empts the charge of grandiosity.
- **Diagnose fragmentation, not absence.** "Nobody owns this question" is a far more defensible claim than "nobody has thought about this," and it is checkable.
- **The naming move** — but only if you can say what changes once the thing is named.
- **Publish outside journals, and expect the ecosystem to be the output.** That is how this movement was built, and it is `00-argument/outlets.md` in one sentence.

## Against me

- **This is the strongest evidence that a diffuse ask produces a diffuse result.** A better-argued essay by more prominent authors, and the output was a movement rather than an institution. If the manifesto wants an institution, it needs Bush's specificity, not this essay's.
- **The partition diagnosis is taken.** Step 3 is already published, in the venue-adjacent literature, by the people who fund the venues being targeted. Any diagnosis in `00-argument/diagnosis.md` has to be checkably different from it.
- **Its factual base is contested.** Do not build on the "declining disruptiveness" literature without engaging the methodological critique.
