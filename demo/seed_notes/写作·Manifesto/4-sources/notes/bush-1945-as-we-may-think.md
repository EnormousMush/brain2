# As We May Think — Vannevar Bush (1945)

> **What this is for me.** The same author, the same year, the other genre. Reading it beside `bush-1945-endless-frontier.md` is the cleanest available demonstration of what an essay can do that a report cannot — and what it cannot do at all.

## How it opens

> "This has not been a scientist's war; it has been a war in which all have had a part. The scientists, burying their old professional competition in the demand of a common cause, have shared greatly and learned much. It has been exhilarating to work in effective partnership. Now, for many, this appears to be approaching an end."

It opens on **loss**, and on a question about what a community should do next. Compare the report, which opens by quoting the letter that commissioned it.

## The essay versus the report — the comparison that matters

| | *Science, The Endless Frontier* | *As We May Think* |
|---|---|---|
| Form | report answering four questions from a president | magazine essay |
| Audience | a few dozen decision-makers | *Atlantic* readers, then *Life*'s millions |
| Ask | a named agency, a governance design, a budget | none — a picture |
| Cost of being wrong | budget authority | **nothing** |
| Produced | the NSF (1950), a permanent budget line | no institution and no funding |
| Verdict at 80 years | the *institution* persists; the *argument* is now attacked | wrong in every technical particular; canonical anyway |

Three things the essay could do that the report could not:

- **Be speculative without political cost.** A report that guesses wrong loses standing. The memex could be wrong about microfilm and lose nothing.
- **Address people with no power.** The report went to a few dozen officials. The essay reached a 20-year-old radar technician named Douglas Engelbart in a Red Cross library on Leyte.
- **Survive by reinterpretability.** Because it committed to an image rather than a claim, each generation could read its own technology into it.

And what the report could do that the essay could not: **create a permanent budget line.**

**Worth noting for the manifesto's own downside case:** Roger Pielke Jr. argues that the *report's* frontier metaphor functioned as an *"intellectual soporific, absolving the scientific community from the need to consider its broader responsibilities"* — borrowing Patty Limerick's term "sedative," a metaphor that "denies consequences and overrules failure." **A frame that propagates can propagate a defect.**

## The memex

> "Consider a future device for individual use, which is a sort of mechanized private file and library. It needs a name, and to coin one at random, 'memex' will do."

> "It consists of a desk, and while it can presumably be operated from a distance, it is primarily the piece of furniture at which he works. On the top are slanting translucent screens."

> "When the user is building a trail, he names it, inserts the name in his code book, and taps it out on his keyboard."

**Specificity: high on mechanism, entirely absent on digital computation.** Storage is microfilm and dry photography; retrieval is keyboard codes, photocells and levers. **He got the substrate completely wrong and the *relation* — associative trails, personal libraries, named paths through information — completely right.**

That asymmetry is the technique. He specified the *behaviour* concretely enough to be recognisable and left the *implementation* to be wrong.

## Downstream effect, with evidence

- **Douglas Engelbart** read it in 1945 as a radar technician and cited it explicitly in his 1962 framework; the mouse, hypertext editing and the 1968 "Mother of All Demos" descend from that programme.
- **Ted Nelson** cited it directly in his 1965 paper coining "hypertext."
- Both are documented citations, not folklore. The lineage runs Bush → Engelbart/Nelson → the modern interface.

## What to steal

- **One concrete image, specified at the level of behaviour, wrong at the level of substrate.** The memex is why the essay survived. **A manifesto about how humanity progresses together needs its memex** — a single concrete, describable, usable thing that makes the abstraction handleable. Without it, there is nothing for a reader to carry away and reinterpret in twenty years.
- **Open on loss and on a live question inside a community**, not on a thesis.
- **Accept that an essay creates no budget line.** If the goal is an institution, the essay is the wrong instrument, and Bush wrote both in the same year because he knew that.

## Against me

- **Its influence was almost entirely on a technical elite.** Two named readers carry most of the causal weight. That is a real form of success and it is not "humanity progressing together."
- **Cost-free speculation is also consequence-free speculation.** Compare `club-of-rome-limits-to-growth.md`, which made falsifiable claims, was attacked by name within a year, and was partially vindicated fifty years later. Bush's essay was never wrong enough to be argued with — which is why it lasted and why it changed no policy.
- **A frame that propagates can propagate its defect.** Pielke on the frontier metaphor is the warning: the durable part of a document is not necessarily the good part.
