# Pugwash

> **The bottleneck.** A Soviet academician and an American presidential science adviser needed a room in which either could explore a position his government had not adopted **without creating a negotiating record.**

The institution the Russell–Einstein Manifesto produced. It is the answer to "what happens after the manifesto works," and the answer is instructive and sobering in equal measure.

## Facts

First conference **7–12 July 1957**, Thinkers' Lodge, **Pugwash, Nova Scotia** — Cyrus Eaton's estate in his birthplace. **22 scientists from 10 countries**: USA 7, USSR 3, Japan 3, UK 2, Canada 2, and one each from Australia, Austria, China, France, Poland. Rotblat was the organising force and edited the proceedings; Eaton financed and hosted.

**Governance.** A "Continuing Committee" between conferences; later a Council of 28 elected every five years, an Executive Committee of six, and a Secretary-General. **No formal membership in the international body.** Participants attend *"in their individual capacities and not as representatives of any organization, institution or government."* Rotblat was Secretary-General 1957–73 and President 1988–97. **This non-representative status is the Manifesto's rhetorical move, institutionalised.**

**Funding.** Eaton financed the founding conference and the early years; **no dollar amounts are published anywhere**, and one philanthropy review notes the absence explicitly. Pugwash later dropped Eaton as sponsor because he was attacked in the US as pro-Soviet. **US Pugwash ran on roughly $20,000–$50,000 a year in the 1960s.** All officers served pro bono; four offices staffed by three full-time people.

**Read that budget again.** An organisation credited with influencing the ABM Treaty ran on less than the cost of one think-tank staffer.

## The mechanism of influence — five channels of very unequal strength

**A. Technical problem-solving that supplies a face-saving formula.** *(Strongest.)* For the 1963 Partial Test Ban Treaty, the blocking issue was verification. **Jerome Wiesner**, Kennedy's incoming science adviser, attended the Moscow Pugwash meeting in late 1960 and discussed inspection numbers with Soviet scientists. Pugwash scientists proposed **"black box" seismic detectors** as an alternative to on-site inspection; **Glenn Seaborg** credits this as "a breakthrough that showed the Soviet side loosening its demands."

The mechanism is not persuasion. **It is the manufacture of a proposal with no national fingerprint on it** — a thing neither government could table first without appearing to concede.

**B. Converting one bloc's scientists, who then convert their own government.** *(Strongest for the ABM Treaty, 1972.)* Matthew Evangelista traces the chain: American physicists persuaded Soviet physicists of a counterintuitive proposition — that missile defence is destabilising because it drives offensive build-up. Soviet scientist **M. D. Millionshchikov** came to oppose ABMs; the view moved through Academy vice-presidents into Kosygin's family circle. Herbert York credits a 1969 Pugwash working group with influencing Soviet positions before SALT was announced.

**Why the counterparties had access:** Soviet participants were vetted and connected — **Millionshchikov, Kirillin and Gvishiani had direct lines to Kosygin and Brezhnev**, and Gvishiani was Kosygin's son-in-law. Rotblat himself acknowledged that some Soviet participants "were obviously sent to push the party line." **That was a feature as much as a bug: they were exactly the people who could carry a message home.** (Gvishiani is the same man who later chaired IIASA's Council for fifteen years — see `iiasa.md`. The cast is small.)

**C. Personnel overlap with official advisory systems.** Not track-two diplomacy in the classic sense — **the same people wearing two hats.** Wiesner and York are the type cases. This also explains the decline: as official arms-control bureaucracies matured, the unofficial channel lost its monopoly.

**D. Literal back-channel courier — documented, and it failed.** In 1967, **Raymond Aubrac** (French Resistance leader, personal friend of Ho Chi Minh) and Pugwashite Herbert Marcovich travelled to Hanoi as intermediaries between Kissinger and McNamara on one side and Ho Chi Minh and Pham Van Dong on the other. US codename **PENNSYLVANIA**. It failed. Aubrac: *"PENNSYLVANIA would have succeeded if it had taken place one year before."* McNamara noted that "the vast majority of war-related fatalities on both sides occurred after the failure of PENNSYLVANIA."

**This is the purest instance of the mechanism and the clearest evidence that the mechanism is weak on its own.** Pugwash could supply trusted private individuals with pre-existing relationships across a hostile divide — an asset no state could manufacture — and it still did not produce peace.

**E. Long-run ideational influence.** Gorbachev requested a Pugwash study group on conventional forces and told them "your analysis was very close to our understanding"; he reportedly told Rotblat that Pugwash scientists were crucial in shaping his views. Wittner credits the broader antinuclear movement while noting Pugwash's "cutting edge role" had diminished by the 1980s.

## Which claims survive scrutiny

Pugwash's official list is PTBT, NPT, ABM, SALT I, BWC, INF, CWC.

| claim | verdict |
|---|---|
| **PTBT 1963** | **well supported** — Wiesner's channel, the seismic formula, Seaborg's own credit |
| **ABM 1972** | **well supported** — Evangelista's traced chain, York's 1969 working group |
| **NPT 1968** | **weak** — Rubinson: "rather little evidence exists to support the NPT claim" |
| **BWC 1972** | **weak/contested** — appears "sporadically at best" in the Pugwash literature |
| **Vietnam** | **overstated**, explicitly |
| **SALT I, INF, CWC** | **unverified** — asserted, no independent assessment found |

British Pugwash's own history page asserts contributions with no named individuals, no specific meetings and no explanation of mechanisms. Worth remembering when weighing any institution's self-account, including a future one.

## Named critics

- **Andrei Sakharov (1988)** — the most damaging, because of who he was. Called Pugwash *"mediocre"* and lacking an *"objective, scholarly approach,"* its scientists *"self-absorbed, without a direct link to government or to the media."*
- **Leo Szilárd** — meetings wasted time "drafting an official public statement" and would achieve more if smaller and more focused.
- **Mark Kramer** — expresses relief Pugwash was not more successful, calling its disarmament goals "deeply misguided."
- **Rubinson** — the Cold War definition of objectivity "limited its scientists to narrow technical arguments." And the sting: **the Test Ban Treaty paradoxically increased nuclear testing**, because the Senate demanded accelerated underground testing as the price of ratification. *The signature success arguably raised the number of tests.*

## Verdict, and the structural reason

**Worked, narrowly, for about three decades. Four structural features:**

1. **Deniability is the product.** Szilárd's description of what he valued — *"informal discussions, in which a man would listen and then respond with a frown or a smile without having to say anything"* — is the entire business model in one sentence. **Formal standing would have destroyed the function.**
2. **Technical framing as a solvent for political deadlock.** Physicists from opposed blocs share a language diplomats do not. Pugwash converted political impasses into technical ones, solved the technical version and handed the result back. Rubinson's critique is the same observation with the sign reversed: that narrowing was also the ceiling on its power.
3. **Selection for dual-hatted people.** Influence was proportional to the fraction of attendees who advised governments.
4. **Continuity.** A conference *series*, not an event. Relationships compounded across decades.

**Status now:** still active — the 63rd conference met in Hiroshima, 1–5 November 2025, marking 80 years since the bombing. But note **an eight-year gap in the flagship activity** between the 62nd (Astana, 2017) and the 63rd; the planned 2020 Doha conference was cancelled for COVID and not rescheduled for five years.

## Against me

- **The moral appeal cashed out as technical consultancy.** The Manifesto asked humanity to remember its humanity; the institution's two best-documented achievements are a seismic detector proposal and a persuasive argument about missile defence stability. **That gap between the register of the document and the register of its effects is the most useful thing in this note** — and a manifesto should expect it.
- **Zero budget, zero standing, and it still worked — but only where the deadlock was technical.** Where the deadlock was political (Vietnam), the same mechanism, with better human assets, failed.
- **The mechanism depended on a bipolar world with two scientific elites who both had Politburo-level access.** The 2026 analogue is not obvious. Frontier AI capability sits in commercial labs, not academies, and the relevant "scientists" are employees with equity.
- **Sakharov's charge is the one to be afraid of:** self-absorbed people with no direct link to government or media, producing statements. That is the default outcome of this genre, and Pugwash escaped it only because Wiesner happened to be in Moscow.

## Implication

**Q3, and the design of the ask.** Pugwash is the proof that bindingness is not required for influence — but it narrows what "influence" means. The transferable lesson: **build the venue where a costly position can be explored without becoming a record.** If the manifesto's institution has one genuinely unavailable capability, that may be it — and unlike a tunnel, it is buildable by people with no money.
