# The 2026 landscape — who is already in this room

**Read this before writing a sentence.** This is not a precedent folder in the usual sense. These are not models to learn from; they are **occupants of the position**. The purpose of this file is to prevent restating something that 193 states, a Pope and a head of state have already said.

The honest headline: **this space is extremely crowded, and it got dramatically more crowded in the eighteen months before the vault was started.** Two documents published in May and July 2026 occupy the exact centre of gravity named in `00-argument/intent.md`.

---

## A. The governance corpus — non-binding instruments, ranked by what they actually did

| document | date | what it asked | what happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Asilomar AI Principles** (FLI) | Jan 2017 | 23 principles, ~1,100 words, no binding ask | Seeded OECD (2019) → G20 (2019) → UNESCO (2021). Its output was **legitimation of a vocabulary**, not compliance. Held at the same venue as `asilomar-1975.md` — an explicit brand borrowing |
| **Montréal Declaration** | Dec 2018 | 10 principles, 59 recommendations | Its distinguishing feature was **method** — citizen co-construction workshops rather than expert fiat. That deliberative move is what later documents copy. Cited historically now, not operationally |
| **UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of AI** | Nov 2021 | 141 paragraphs, 11 policy areas | **Adopted by all 193 member states** — the only universal AI instrument. Binding force: none; a "recommendation" obliges states only to submit it to national authorities and report periodically. **75+ states engaged with the Readiness Assessment Methodology; ~60 completed diagnoses.** The gap between "193 adopted" and "nothing changed" is itself a usable fact |
| **FLI "Pause Giant AI Experiments"** | 22 Mar 2023 | ≥6-month pause on training beyond GPT-4; government moratorium failing that | **31,810 signatures** as displayed Aug 2026. No pause; capex rose an order of magnitude. But it is the **single most consequential agenda-setting document of the period** — it converted AI risk into a mainstream political object within weeks |
| **CAIS Statement on AI Risk** | 30 May 2023 | one sentence, 22 words | *"Mitigating the risk of extinction from AI should be a global priority alongside other societal-scale risks such as pandemics and nuclear war."* Deliberately single-sentence to deny critics surface area. Its function was to establish that **frontier-lab CEOs themselves endorse the extinction framing** — which is what made Bletchley possible. Cost to signatories: zero |
| **Bletchley Declaration** | 1–2 Nov 2023 | ~1,200 words; frontier AI poses catastrophic risk | 28 countries + EU, **including the US and China**. China's signature was the achievement |
| **Seoul** | 21–22 May 2024 | Frontier AI Safety Commitments | 27 countries + EU; **16 companies** pledged to publish safety frameworks. Launched the AI Safety Institute network |
| **Paris** | 10–11 Feb 2025 | "Inclusive and Sustainable AI for People and the Planet" | **58 signatories; the US and UK refused.** Deliberate pivot from "safety" to "action." Launched Current AI (~$400M) |
| **New Delhi Declaration on AI Impact** | 16–21 Feb 2026 | three "Sutras" — People, Planet, Progress | **~88 countries, including both the US and China.** >100 countries, 20+ heads of state. Non-binding, no verification. Deliverables include a **Global AI Impact Commons** and >$250bn in infrastructure pledges |
| **International AI Safety Report** (Bengio-chaired) | 2025, 2026 editions | evidence synthesis | 96 experts, 30 countries. Second full report **3 Feb 2026**. The US reportedly withheld support for the 2026 edition |

**Two drift facts worth more than the whole table.** First, the summit series was renamed twice: *AI Safety Summit* → *AI Action Summit* → *AI Impact Summit*. Second, **"safety" was removed from the names of both national institutes within eighteen months of Bletchley** — the UK AI Safety Institute became the AI **Security** Institute (Feb 2025), and the US AI Safety Institute became the Center for AI **Standards and Innovation** (Jun 2025).

**A document about how humanity progresses together, written in 2026, is writing after that drift and should account for it.**

## B. The essayistic corpus — the genre this manifesto is entering

- **Dario Amodei** — see `amodei-essays.md`. Two essays, October 2024 and January 2026, now occupy both the optimistic and pessimistic halves of the argument. **Whatever the manifesto says, it will be positioned relative to him.**
- **Vitalik Buterin, d/acc** ("My techno-optimism," Nov 2023; "d/acc: one year later," Jan 2025). Rejects both accelerationist and doomer poles; **defensive / decentralised / differential** acceleration — favour technology that raises the cost of coercion. **Critique: it is a taxonomy more than a programme**; offence/defence classification is contested for nearly every real technology.
- **Marc Andreessen, "The Techno-Optimist Manifesto"** (Oct 2023). ~5,200 words, aphoristic litany, named enemies including "existential risk" and "tech ethics," patron saints including Marinetti. **The negative example of the genre:** manifesto-as-litany with named enemies produces coalition, not conviction — and it succeeded at exactly that, becoming the founding text of a political coalition that later shaped US AI policy.
- **Nick Bostrom, *Deep Utopia*** (Mar 2024). Post-scarcity → post-instrumental → post-meaning. **Not a competitor — a citation.** It occupies the terminal-state slot; a manifesto about the transition is arguing about a different object.
- **e/acc.** Mainstreamed and simultaneously dissolved. **Assume the accelerationist position is occupied at the level of policy and vacant at the level of prose.**
- **Mustafa Suleyman, *The Coming Wave*** (2023) — the **containment problem**: we can neither stop the wave nor safely let it run. And "Towards Humanist Superintelligence" (Nov 2025) — deliberately bounded, domain-specific, subordinated superintelligence. Critique: a corporate positioning document; "humanist" does no load-bearing philosophical work.
- **Reid Hoffman & Greg Beato, *Superagency*** (Jan 2025). AI grants ordinary people amplified capability. **Critique: systematically avoids the power question** — agency for individuals says nothing about who owns the models.
- **Kevin Kelly** — the technium, protopia. **The best tonal precedent available**: gentle, non-tribal optimism, the least politically contested voice in the space. Weakness: determinism.
- **Yuval Noah Harari, *Nexus*** (Sept 2024). History as information networks; AI as the first technology that generates ideas and makes decisions itself; democracies survive via self-correcting mechanisms. **Harari owns the "civilizational-scale narrative about AI" slot in the general-reader market — which is this manifesto's market.**

### The frontier-lab manifesto cycle, and its meta-critique

Sam Altman, "The Intelligence Age" (Sept 2024) and "The Gentle Singularity" (Jun 2025 — *we are already past the event horizon*). Mark Zuckerberg, "Personal Superintelligence" (Jul 2025). Amodei, twice.

**Axios ran the meta-piece in September 2025**, cataloguing the genre and rating each entry on a "utopia meter." Its thesis: *"When your industry requires hundreds of billions of dollars in new investment, you may have no choice but to promise paradise."*

**Assume every reader of this manifesto has absorbed that framing.** It is the ambient cynicism the document is written into, and it needs an answer inside the first thousand words.

### The 2025–26 counter-canon — the strongest writing in the space

| work | who | date | claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| **"AI as Normal Technology"** | Arvind Narayanan & Sayash Kapoor | Apr 2025 | AI is a general-purpose technology whose diffusion is bounded by institutions, not a species-level event. Superintelligence framing is empirically weak and politically dangerous. **The most serious intellectual opposition to this entire genre. If the manifesto cannot answer it, the manifesto is unsafe** |
| **"Gradual Disempowerment"** | Kulveit, Douglas, Ammann, Turan, Krueger, Duvenaud | Jan 2025 | Even *aligned* AI can be catastrophic: as economies, states and cultures stop depending on humans, human influence erodes without anyone deciding it should. **The most rigorous existing statement of the failure mode "together" is meant to prevent** |
| **"The Intelligence Curse"** | Luke Drago & Rudolf Laine | Apr 2025 | Resource-curse logic applied to AGI: once elites no longer need human labour, the incentive to maintain broad welfare disappears. Programme: **Avert / Diffuse / Democratize** |
| **"Superintelligence Strategy"** | Hendrycks, Schmidt, Wang | Mar 2025 | **MAIM** — Mutual Assured AI Malfunction — as a deterrence equilibrium |
| ***Empire of AI*** | Karen Hao | May 2025 | AI development is structurally **colonial**: extraction of data, land, water, energy and cheap labour from periphery to centre. **The most influential critical book in the space and the direct antagonist of "together" whenever incumbents say it** |
| ***If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies*** | Yudkowsky & Soares | Sept 2025 | NYT bestseller. The doom position now has a mass-market text |
| **FLI Statement on Superintelligence** | FLI | 22 Oct 2025 | Calls for prohibition on developing superintelligence pending scientific consensus and public buy-in. **71,603 signatures**, deliberately transpartisan |

## C. The two documents that occupy this manifesto's exact position

Both are 2026. Both are covered in their own notes.

- **`magnifica-humanitas-2026.md`** — Pope Leo XIV's encyclical on AI, ~42,300 words, May 2026. Humanity as one family; solidarity, subsidiarity, the common good; digital colonisation; **Tower of Babel versus Nehemiah's wall.**
- **`waico-2026.md`** — Xi Jinping's WAIC keynote, Shanghai, 17 July 2026, announcing the **World Artificial Intelligence Cooperation Organization**. "Together" is now a great-power geopolitical instrument with an intergovernmental body attached.

## D. The eight saturated claims

**Do not make any of these as if it were new.**

1. **"AI could compress a century of progress into a decade."** Amodei owns the phrase.
2. **"AI is a civilizational inflection point."** Every document above opens this way.
3. **"The benefits must reach everyone, not just rich countries."** Said by Amodei, Altman, Suleyman, UNESCO (193 states), Paris (58 states), New Delhi (~88 states), Xi Jinping and the Pope. **This sentence has been adopted by near-universal state consensus. It is now content-free unless a mechanism is attached.**
4. **"Governance must be international because the risks are global."**
5. **"The real danger is concentration of power, not just misalignment."** Bengio, Hao, FLI's grant programme, the Intelligence Curse, Gradual Disempowerment, the encyclical — **and Amodei himself.**
6. **"Avoid both doomerism and blind acceleration."** This exact triangulation is d/acc (2023), Hoffman's "Bloomer" (2025) and Suleyman's "humanist superintelligence" (2025). **The single most occupied rhetorical position in the field.**
7. **"Meaning does not come from work, so post-labour humans will be fine."**
8. **"AI development is a new colonialism."** Hao owns it; the Pope ratified it.

## The three structural traps

- **The credibility trap.** Any 2026 optimistic manifesto is read through the Axios utopia-meter lens by default. Answer "why is this not marketing?" early and explicitly.
- **The geopolitics trap.** "Humanity together" is no longer neutral. It is China's declared AI-governance brand, and it is the framing the US rejected at Paris and again in the February 2026 UN panel vote. **A manifesto about togetherness that does not name this reads as either naïve or aligned.**
- **The epistemic-exhaustion trap.** The most-read AI writing of 2026 has reportedly been fiction presented as fact. Assertion without mechanism reads as content marketing.

## E. Where the ground is actually unoccupied — five openings, ranked

**1. The mechanism gap between "benefits should be shared" and any account of how. — Strongest by a wide margin.**

Every document above states the aspiration and none supplies a theory. Amodei assigns roughly even odds to global propagation of health benefits and offers no mechanism. Brookings' critique of the *papal encyclical* is that it prevents harm rather than distributing gain. UNESCO has 193 adopters, 75 readiness assessments and zero enforcement. New Delhi is explicitly non-binding with no verification body. **A serious, specific, argued theory of distribution — who pays, what the transfer instrument is, what the enforcement is, and what happens when it fails — does not exist in the persuasive literature.** It is a real hole and it sits exactly on the stated centre of gravity.

**2. "Together" as a constructive thesis rather than a negative one.**

The rigorous work on collective humanity in the AI context — Gradual Disempowerment, the Intelligence Curse — is framed entirely as *loss*: what we forfeit when humanity stops functioning as a collective agent. **Nobody has written the positive counterpart.** Global-justice philosophy (Pogge, Caney, cosmopolitanism) is a mature literature and has barely been applied to AI. **This is where a philosophical manifesto, as opposed to a policy document, could do work nobody else is doing.**

**3. The secular translation of the encyclical's argument.**

*Magnifica Humanitas* has the best available vocabulary for humanity-as-one and it is unusable outside the Catholic frame. There is no secular equivalent with comparable moral architecture. **The Babel-versus-Nehemiah distinction — concentrated homogenised control against distributed community-accountable building — is a genuinely good frame currently locked inside a religious document.**

**4. Compute asymmetry as philosophy rather than as policy.**

The numbers are public and devastating: **only 32 countries host specialised AI data centres; 150+ have none. The US and China control over 90% of specialised compute. US firms run 87 major hubs, Chinese firms 39, European firms 6. Harvard's Kempner Institute alone holds more compute than all African-owned AI facilities combined.** The academic framing — **"Compute North vs Compute South"** (Lehdonvirta, Wu, Hawkins) — exists in Oxford papers and UN foresight reports and **has not been metabolised into a moral argument by anyone writing for a general audience.** It is a phrase awaiting an essayist.

**5. Language and cultural fragmentation.**

Named in the intent, and almost nobody in this corpus addresses it. The literature covers nation, compute and income; **the linguistic and epistemic dimension of who models are built for is comparatively unwritten in this genre.** A small opening, and a real one — and one where first-hand material from two language ecosystems is an actual asset.

*(Note before using the phrase "Global South": Stanford HAI has published an issue brief arguing against the term in AI ethics and policy. Read it first.)*

## F. Three things to read before drafting

1. **Amodei, "Machines of Loving Grace" and "The Adolescence of Technology," back to back.** He has now written both halves of the argument being entered.
2. **Narayanan & Kapoor, "AI as Normal Technology."** The strongest argument that this whole genre is a category error.
3. **The Babel/Nehemiah sections of *Magnifica Humanitas*, and Xi's WAICO speech.** The two direct competitors on the word "together" — one with a moral architecture, one with an intergovernmental organisation.

## The one-line verdict

**Do not write a manifesto arguing that AI's benefits should reach all of humanity. That claim has been made by 193 states, the Pope, two frontier-lab CEOs and the President of China, and is now worth nothing. Write the manifesto that says *how* — and that takes seriously the possibility that "together" is not achievable by exhortation. The mechanism, not the aspiration, is the unoccupied ground.**
