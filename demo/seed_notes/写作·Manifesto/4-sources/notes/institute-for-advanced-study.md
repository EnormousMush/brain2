# Institute for Advanced Study

> **The bottleneck.** A first-rank scholar could hold a permanent position with no teaching, no students, no committees and no directed research — an arrangement no university would offer and no funder would underwrite.

Paired with `flexner-1939-useless-knowledge.md`, which is the argument this institution was built to embody.

## Facts

Incorporated **20 May 1930** in New Jersey on a **$5M founding gift** from **Louis Bamberger and Caroline Bamberger Fuld**, proceeds of their Newark department store. Founding director **Abraham Flexner**, 1930–39.

**Founding language, which is unusually explicit about design:** the Institute should be **"small and plastic,"** provide **"complete intellectual liberty"** and freedom **"from administrative responsibilities,"** and permit work **"without being carried off in the maelstrom of the immediate."**

**Structural decisions:** no students, no degree programmes, no classes to teach, **no experimental laboratories**, no directed research, no committees. Permanent Faculty plus rotating Members.

**Sequencing, which is the shrewdest thing about it:** the first school, announced autumn 1932, was the **School of Mathematics** — the cheapest school (no labs) and the one where the recruitable global talent pool was most concentrated, at a moment when European scholars were beginning to leave. Inaugural faculty **Oswald Veblen and Albert Einstein**; by 1933–34 Alexander, von Neumann and Weyl, plus twenty-odd visitors; Dirac and Pauli in 1934–35.

**Present scale:** ~25 permanent faculty, ~190 visiting members a year from 100+ institutions, four schools. Endowment funds ~75% of operating expenses; the founders' $5M is worth more than $200M in today's terms. Claimed among past and present faculty and members: 37 Nobel laureates, 46 of 64 Fields Medalists, 25 of 29 Abel Prize laureates.

## Scope: how wide, and what holds it there

Four schools, and nothing enforces anything — but note that the schools do not *need* to converge, because the institute's product is individual scholarship. Breadth here is a portfolio of parallel excellences, not an integrative claim. **When the manifesto cites the IAS, it should be clear which of the two it is proposing.**

## Kill mechanism

None. Permanent faculty appointments with no renewal review. This is the deliberate opposite of Janelia and it is the specific feature Feynman's critique targets.

## Verdict, and the structural reason

**Mixed.** The recruitment and prize record is extraordinary and largely a product of timing plus an endowment plus the removal of teaching duties. The countervailing evidence is the persistent insider claim that the arrangement is not optimal for *output*.

**Richard Feynman**, *Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman!*, "The Dignified Professor" — the canonical statement:

> "These poor bastards could now sit and think clearly all by themselves, OK? So they don't get an idea for a while: They have every opportunity to do something, and they're not getting any ideas. I believe that in a situation like this a kind of guilt or depression worms inside of you."

Feynman's structural claim is that removing all obligation removes the **friction** — teaching, students, concrete problems — that generates problems worth solving. **Freedom from duty is not the same as freedom to produce.** Whether or not one accepts it, it is a *structural* explanation and therefore admissible here.

## Against me

- **This is the institution the manifesto will be accused of proposing.** Well-endowed, permanent, unconstrained, for the greatest minds, on the hardest problems. The accusation has a canonical rebuttal attached to it. Pre-empt it.
- **Its founding sequencing is worth stealing and its steady state is not.** Start where the talent pool is concentrated and the capital cost is lowest; do not conclude that permanence without review is the model.
- **Flexner's closing move — refusing to defend the Institute on utility grounds — immunises it against evaluation.** Elegant, and every subsequent claim that a research institute cannot be measured traces back to that paragraph. A manifesto that adopts it forfeits the ability to say whether the institution worked.

## Implication

**Q2, and the kill mechanism.** The IAS versus Janelia comparison is the cleanest natural experiment available on whether unconditional permanence or reviewed terms produce more. Both are well-funded, both removed grant-writing, and they differ mainly in whether anything ever ends.
