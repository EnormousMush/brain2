# Thread — capacity, not outputs

The likeliest original contribution. Also the one most at risk of already existing.

## The distinction

"The benefits of AI" is at least five things with five distributive logics:

1. **Outputs** — a diagnosis, a forecast, a translation.
2. **Access** — the ability to run the model.
3. **Capability** — the ability to build, adapt and evaluate.
4. **Agenda-setting** — the ability to determine what gets worked on.
5. **The standing to refuse** — declining a deployment without bearing the cost.

**The claim:** the consensus is entirely about (1) and (2), because "benefits" means outputs. And **distributing outputs and distributing capacity are not two amounts of the same thing** — one leaves the recipient a consumer of answers to questions someone else chose; the other changes who gets to ask.

## Why it might be the contribution

It reframes the saturated claim without contradicting it. Everyone agrees benefits should be shared; this says the agreement is about the wrong *object* — a different kind of disagreement, much harder to absorb into a communiqué.

## Why it might not be

- **Sen's capability approach is the obvious relative.** If it has been applied to AI in this exact way, the contribution shifts to what follows from the distinction. **Check before claiming novelty (C8).**
- "Capacity building," "AI sovereignty," "compute sovereignty" are adjacent and in circulation.

## The task that settles it

**The audit (C7).** Classify ten consensus documents against the five categories. If it comes out as claimed, the table is original evidence and goes in the paper. If not, the essay learns something more valuable than it expected. **A bounded weekend; the highest-value single task in the vault.**
