# Provocations

Questions designed to break a stall. Not rhetorical — each is meant to be answered in writing, badly, quickly.

**On "together"**
- If every person on earth had free access to the best model tomorrow, would the problem be solved? If not, what remains? *(The fastest test of whether you mean sense 1 or sense 2.)*
- Is there a version of "together" a species can achieve without agreeing on anything?
- What is the smallest unit that can be "together" — a species, a generation, a set of institutions?

**On the diagnosis**
- Name the person who is worse off if the mechanism gets built. If nobody, the problem is not structural and will solve itself.
- What is the most charitable case that the consensus *is* the mechanism — norms cascade, and it is simply early?
- Was there ever a technology whose benefits *did* distribute broadly? What did the mechanism look like? Did anyone design it?

**On capacity**
- Is a country that can fine-tune but not pre-train "participating in production"? Where is the line, and does it move every eighteen months?
- If capacity is the good, what is the unit? A researcher? A data centre? A curriculum? An institution?
- Who currently *has* the standing to refuse an AI deployment? Anyone?

**On the essay itself**
- If you had to cut it to 800 words, what survives? *(Russell–Einstein was 2,280.)*
- What is the one sentence someone would quote?
- What would the strongest reviewer say — the one who shares your values and thinks the argument is weak?
- If this essay had already been written by someone else, what would you wish it had said?

**On being stuck**
- Are you stuck because the argument is wrong, or because it is right and hard to say?
- What did you believe about this a year ago that you no longer believe? That gap is usually where the essay is.
