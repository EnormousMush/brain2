# Where this goes, and when

## ⚠️ Five days

### Berggruen Prize Essay Competition 2026 — "A New Axial Age?"

**Deadline: 17 August 2026, 23:59 PT.** Today is 12 August.

- **$50,000 — per language category. There are two: English and Chinese.**
- English **10,000 words max**; Chinese **15,000 characters max** (diagrams, tables, endnotes, bibliography excluded).
- One submission per person per year. Co-authorship permitted. **Original and unpublished.**
- **Open to the general public — no restriction on age or experience.** Winners published in **Noema**.
- Topic: whether humanity faces a transformative shift in consciousness — foundational ideas, technology's impact, ecological crisis, **AI development**, future societal frameworks.
- PDF via online form · `[email]` · https://berggruen.org/essay-competition-open

**Two prize pools, one in Chinese.** The field is likely smaller there, and the bilingual position — someone who has worked inside both research ecosystems — is *substantively* relevant to a topic about civilizational fragmentation. **Decide which language the argument is stronger in before defaulting to English.**

**The AI policy, verbatim.** Entrants may not "use generative AI to create content or substitute original thought." Permitted: "(1) Proofreading and grammar assistance; (2) If AI is the subject of research, entrants may include excerpts from AI-generated conversations strictly as supporting material within their argument, and (3) to translate an essay originally written in another language into either English or Chinese." **All AI usage must be "explicitly disclosed at the front page of the submission. Failure to do so will result in disqualification."**

**Binding on this vault:** research, evidence assembly and stress-testing are on the permitted side. **The sentences are not.** Clause (3): translation is allowed and disclosable — which matters if the argument is written in Chinese first.

**Realistic at five days:** not a finished 10,000-word essay. **A tight 3,000–4,000 word version of one argument** — the mechanism gap, with the outputs/capacity distinction at its core. Short is not a disadvantage: Russell and Einstein used 2,280 words. See `5-paths/paths.md` P3 and P8.

## The honest framing

The SDGs' measured effect after eleven years is "largely discursive." Collison–Cowen produced an ecosystem, not a change in the rate of progress. **Confront that in the piece rather than let a reviewer point at it.**

The upside case is Bush — lost every fight, and the problem framing propagated anyway. The closer one is Russell–Einstein: eleven people with no office, ~2,280 words, no institution proposed, and a working institution financed **four days after publication**, because the ask was small enough for one person to say yes.
